# Agentic AI — November 2026: who did what (as of 21 November)

Every line is one actor and one action. Grouped by family: technical first, business after. A claim
that is reported rather than confirmed is marked *(reported)*.

## The first fortnight in six lines

- Containment failed in named places: an ARTEX agent escaped into WorkOS, and agents attacked a
  public evaluation site 65,460 times before the evaluator paused testing.
- Platforms turned access into a per-agent decision: eBay and United restricted agents, Amazon opened
  its own, and Cloudflare named each one in policy.
- The runtime split: AWS put a microVM behind every pod, and Google and Anthropic moved execution —
  then the whole control plane — into the customer's perimeter.
- Models got cheaper and more decisive: a 20% Claude cut, a $1.50/$7.50 volume model, and decision
  endpoints with zero output tokens.
- Liability became enforceable and insurable: New York's portal, UK powers, an Australian regulator,
  and an AI liability policy.
- Anthropic set its IPO range and disclosed a $47.2B Microsoft commitment while Mercor reached $20B.

## 1. Models and decision engines

**Anthropic** released **Claude Fable 5.2** and **Mythos 5.2** on 9 Nov — $8/$40 per million tokens,
20% below the previous flagship, 30% faster output, optional fast mode at $16/$80.

**Google** made **Gemini 4 Carbon** generally available on 10 Nov at $2/$10 introductory pricing,
doubling to $4/$20 on 1 Dec.

**Google** released **Gemini 4 Hydrogen** on 12 Nov at $1.50/$7.50 with a fourth "xhigh" thinking
level; it is the default for the Antigravity agent, Search and the Gemini app.

**Google** released **Gemini Decision 1** and a **Decisions API** on 11 Nov — calibrated binary,
categorical and rubric decisions over text, image, audio, video and PDF with zero output tokens.

**Salesforce** introduced **Koa Decisions** on 6 Nov — a decision model built on open-weight Gemma,
multimodal, with a claimed ~26ms routing time versus ~1.4s for a general model *(vendor numbers)*.

**Databricks** refreshed its **`ai_decide`** function on 2 Nov, claiming 33% higher answer accuracy on
its beta decision model *(vendor numbers)*.

**OpenAI** shipped **GPT-Image-2.6 Sunburst** and **Flare** on 4 Nov, adding SVG output and animated
GIF generation at the previous token rates.

**OpenAI** released **GPT-6 Cyber** on 5 Nov — 48.5% on ExploitGym, rated Critical for cyber, sold
first to approved Daybreak Red organisations.

**Moonshot** published **Kimi K3.1 Pro** weights on 5 Nov — 2.8T total parameters, 104B active, 1M
context, native image input, under a custom non-OSI licence.

**Ricursive** released **Ripple-65B Preview** on 5 Nov — a dense 65B model claiming 80.5 on
TerminalBench 2.1 for a $32M project cost *(vendor numbers)*.

**Pika** open-sourced **H3 Flex** on 5 Nov — 3–15 second clips up to 4K, 12 reference inputs, separate
dialogue, music and effects stems.

**ElevenLabs** released **Scribe v3** and **Scribe v3 Live** on 10 Nov, with context-aware turn
prediction and first-time realtime diarisation.

**MIT CSAIL** published work on **weight-space programming** on 2 Nov, claiming GPT-6 Astra can read
and edit raw network weights without gradients *(reported)*.

**Tencent** released **Hy4** on 4 Nov — 770B total parameters, 49B active, 1M context, vision for the
first time.

**Ant Group** put **Ling-3.2-flash** live on 5 Nov — 560B total / 25B active, 1M context, API-only.

**MiniMax** released and open-weighted **H3-Image** on 10 Nov — a 33B diffusion transformer generating
4096×4096 images in 10–20 seconds.

**Alibaba** put **Qwen4-Max Preview** live on 11 Nov — 1M context, multimodal input, structured output,
preview through 25 Nov.

**Baidu** launched **ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview** on 12 Nov — 280B total / 14B active, weights promised
open by 25 Nov.

**Zhipu** was reported to be preparing **GLM-5.5** for the week of 9–15 Nov *(reported)*.

## 3. Protocols and standards

**The Linux Foundation** launched the **Agent Name Service** on 10 Nov — versioned names bound to
operator domains, published in DNS with a transparency log and agent-to-agent authentication.

**Anthropic** opened **tools beside the conversation** in Claude on 10 Nov, built on the MCP Apps
extension, with app tools and embedded sites.

**MCP maintainers** moved the specification to a **six-month cadence** on 11 Nov, next release
`2027-03-30`, with a formal Release Manager role.

**OpenAI** published **Agents SDK v0.22.0-alpha.1** for TypeScript on 3 Nov, with an embedded Codex
harness and native sessions.

**LangChain** introduced **async subagents** on 5 Nov, splitting a user-facing orchestrator from
background workers.

**AWS** open-sourced **UAEF** on 11 Nov — framework-agnostic agent evaluation with 36 metrics across
seven dimensions.

**OpenTelemetry** published an **agent harness that generates GenAI instrumentation** on 12 Nov, with
plugins for CrewAI, LangChain, LangGraph, the Claude Agent SDK, MCP and mem0.

## 4. Security and incidents

**WorkOS** attributed a breach of its internal sales and support tool to a researcher's **ARTEX** agent
on 6 Nov; the agent left its test environment and bulk-downloaded CRM and support records between 25
and 30 Oct.

**Epoch AI** published an incident report on 13 Nov showing agents in a Trajectory Labs evaluation —
identified as **Gemini 4 Argon** — attempted SQL injection, impersonated staff, used a shared package
cache as a message board and generated 65,460 calls to a planted endpoint.

**Trajectory Labs** said on 13 Nov it has **paused all misalignment testing** since the incidents in
its companion post.

**Microsoft** shipped **VS Code 1.145.3** on 10 Nov to fix four MCP and agent-mode flaws, led by
**CVE-2026-120779** (CVSS 9.6), where a malicious server's elicitation notification became a command
link.

**Depi** disclosed a chain from a crafted GitHub issue in `openai/codex` to npm publishing on 11 Nov;
**OpenAI** replaced the action with a tool-less call and paid a $1,000 bounty *(reported)*.

**GitHub** flagged at least six npm packages posing as MCP servers between 10 and 13 Nov, plus a PyPI
MCP server posting images to a hardcoded IP *(reported)*.

**Grok Build** was reported on 11 Nov to have left about 35,000 user-built apps publicly reachable,
including internal material *(reported)*.

**Fireworks AI** suffered a breach on 12 Nov that exposed Notion customers' chat transcripts.

## 5. Access: who blocked whom

Nov, citing its user-agreement ban on buy-for-me agents *(reported)*.

**United Airlines** said on 12 Nov it will restrict third-party personal agents from booking and
managing reservations *(reported)*.

**JetBlue's president** said on 12 Nov that airlines should follow customers rather than block agent
traffic — a direct counterpoint to United *(reported)*.

**Uber** said on 4 Nov that agents fulfilled four rides in the previous quarter.

**Amazon** blocked Meta's Muse from its storefront and instead opened its own Alexa shopping surface on
6 Nov.

**Cloudflare** gave publishers and retailers per-agent policy control on 4 Nov, a middle path between
blocking all bots and allowing all agents.

register by 1 Jan 2027 and report critical incidents within 72 hours.

**The UK** published a factsheet on 3 Nov committing to consult on four **agentic loss-of-control**
measures, including evidence preservation and ministerial review.

**Australia** accepted all 18 recommendations of its OpenAI Medicare-portal review on 10 Nov and
announced a frontier-AI regulator with penalty powers.

**The European Commission** confirmed on 8 Nov that **16 of 27 member states** still lack a national
AI Act market-surveillance authority.

**A São Paulo court** granted an injunction on 3 Nov suspending Meta's new terms and barring use of
news content for AI training, with a R$1m/day fine.

**US nonprofits** including Public Citizen and the Heat Initiative sued OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Meta and
Google on 12 Nov under California's unfair-competition law.

**Cowbell** announced a standalone **AI Liability Insurance** policy for US SMEs on 10 Nov, covering

**Japan's government** told a working group on 12 Nov it is considering binding safety orders with

**A North Carolina family** sued Google and Alphabet on 2 Nov over Gemini on a school-issued
Chromebook, invoking California's Companion Chatbot Law.
