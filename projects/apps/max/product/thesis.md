# Thesis

## What Max is

A smart home app built around decisions, not controls. The home is the subject: what it owns, what
it is doing, what will fail, and what should happen next. Max makes the call, says why, and keeps a
record.

The product is three things in one shell:

1. **A feed of decisions** — what Max noticed, what it did, what it needs from you.
2. **A record of the home** — devices, manuals, warranties, repairs, outcomes.
3. **A control layer** — present, calm, and third in importance.

## What it is not

- Not a control grid. Turning on a light is commodity and free with every platform.
- Not a care app. It is not built around maintenance as a service; maintenance is one decision domain.
- Not a chatbot with devices attached. The conversation is one input, not the product.
- Not a care-robot or hardware play. Software only.

## Where the value sits

By 2030 the control layer is free and the assistant is bundled with the hardware. What stays scarce
is **authority, evidence and attention**:

- who is allowed to act in a physical place, and within what limits;
- who can prove what happened, to an insurer, a manufacturer or a buyer;
- who decides what is worth interrupting a person for.

Max competes on those three, and on the depth of what it does when something breaks.

## The 2030 bets, in order of reachability

1. **The home record** — portable, signed, owned by the household, not by a platform.
2. **The failure-to-fix graph** — which step fixed which fault on which model, learned from outcomes.
3. **Delegated authority** — budgets, scopes, approvals, an audit trail a third party accepts.
4. **Energy as a market** — shift load, use cheap or green hours, participate in flexibility.
5. **Agent-to-agent** — negotiate with manufacturer, insurer, utility and technician agents.
6. **The attention protocol** — an interruption budget with rules, not a do-not-disturb toggle.
7. **Care and embodied help** — the highest-value vertical, and the hardest.

## The positioning sentence

Other apps let you control a house. Max decides what the house should do, shows its reasoning, and
remembers the outcome.

## What has to be true

- The decisions are better than a rule the user could have written.
- The reasoning is legible enough to trust and cheap enough to produce.
- The record is worth keeping after the novelty of the app wears off.
